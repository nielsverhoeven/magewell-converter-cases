# Noctua Fans for Magewell Pro Convert Cases

Reference data for selecting a Noctua cooling fan for a 3D-printed protective case housing a
Magewell Pro Convert NDI video converter (typ. 5–15 W dissipation). All figures below are taken
directly from Noctua's own product pages (noctua.at) unless otherwise noted. Any figure that could
not be verified from a fetched source is marked `unknown` — it is never estimated or inferred.

All prices are approximate **street prices in EUR**, taken from Amazon.de listings (fetched
2026-09-07) unless otherwise noted; actual prices vary by retailer/region and over time.

## Accessories

### NA-AV3 anti-vibration mounts (sold as the **NA-SAV3** 16-piece set)

- **What it is:** a set of 16 silicone anti-vibration mounts (replaces fan screws), enough for up
  to 4–5 fans.
- **Material:** silicone rubber (brown).
- **Fan compatibility / how it works:** the NA-AV3 is a *double-sided* mount with **5 numbered
  pawls**. Pawl 1 works with standard open-corner fans of any thickness; pawls 2–5 are sized for
  fans with **closed corners at 10, 14, 15, 20 or 25 mm thickness** — i.e. it is designed to fit
  slim fans (like the NF-A4x10/NF-A4x20) where single-sided mounts don't seat well. Installation:
  push the mounts through the fan's mounting holes first, then push them through the case's
  mounting holes (opposite order from single-sided mounts like NA-AV1/NA-AV4).
- **Effect:** decouples the fan from the case structure, reducing structure-borne (transmitted)
  vibration noise — relevant for a rigid FDM-printed enclosure where a hard-screwed fan can couple
  motor vibration into the whole shell.
- **Important:** per the fetched scope-of-delivery data above, the NF-A4x10 and NF-A4x20 families
  ship with NA-AV3 mounts, but the **NF-A6x25 and NF-A8x25 families ship with NA-AV1** (single-sided)
  mounts instead — NA-AV3 is not universally bundled with every Noctua fan.
- **Price:** ≈€9.90 for the 16-piece NA-SAV3 set.
- Sources: [NA-SAV3 spec sheet][na-sav3], [installation FAQ][av-faq]

### NA-FC1 fan controller

- **What it does:** a compact inline controller for up to **three 4-pin PWM fans**. A rotary dial
  lets you manually set a PWM duty cycle from 0–100%, either standalone or combined with an
  incoming PWM signal from elsewhere (it can reduce, not increase, the duty cycle a
  motherboard/host would otherwise supply). A push-button "no-stop" mode prevents the fan from
  dropping below ~300 rpm.
- **Voltage range:** 5 – 12 V. **Max current:** 3 A. **Max power:** 36 W. Connector: 4-pin PWM.
  Size: 21 × 25 × 48 mm.
- **Use case here:** since it needs a **PWM** fan to work (non-PWM 3-pin fans cannot be speed
  controlled by it), it's only relevant if a PWM variant is chosen; it gives independent,
  motherboard-free manual speed/noise control — useful for a standalone touring case powered from
  a bench/USB supply where you want to dial the fan down for near-silent operation when the
  converter is running cool.
- **Scope of delivery:** NA-FC1 controller, NA-SC1 3-way splitter cable, NA-EC1 input cable, NA-AC4
  input cable with power-supply adaptor.
- **Price:** ≈€24.90.
- Source: [NA-FC1 spec sheet][na-fc1]

### OmniJoin adaptor set

- **Purpose:** lets you connect a Noctua fan (which ships with a standard 2-pin/3-pin/4-pin
  connector) to a device's **proprietary, non-standard fan header** — exactly the kind of
  situation a Magewell converter's internal fan header (if repurposed) or a fully custom
  case-internal wiring harness would present. Usage: cut the original fan's cable near its
  connector, splice/crimp it onto the OmniJoin adaptor using the supplied connectors, then plug
  the Noctua fan into the (now OmniJoin-terminated) proprietary header.
- **Availability:** it is not a standalone purchasable product — it's bundled free with many
  (not all) Noctua fans; check each fan's "scope of delivery" (all four NF-A4x10 variants and both
  A4x20/A6x25 5V-family fans reviewed above include it, per their own spec pages listed above).
- Source: [OmniJoin tech page][omnijoin]

### NA-EC1 extension cable (sold as the **NA-SEC1** 3-piece set)

- **Length:** 30 cm per cable. **Connector:** 4-pin (compatible with both 3-pin and 4-pin PWM
  fans). Cables can be daisy-chained if more than 30 cm of reach is needed.
- **Price:** ≈€9.90 for a set of 3 (NA-SEC1).
- Source: [NA-SEC1 spec sheet][na-sec1]

### General 5V-from-USB power options

- **Noctua's own solution:** Noctua calls its bundled cable the **NA-AC7 USB power cable**
  (USB Type-A to the fan's native 2-pin/3-pin connector, via the OmniJoin adaptor). Per Noctua's
  own FAQ, this cable is included **only with some 5V fan SKUs** — you must check each fan's scope
  of delivery. Confirmed from the data gathered above:
  - **Included in box:** NF-A4x10 5V PWM, NF-A8 5V, and (per Amazon listing text) NF-A8 5V PWM.
  - **NOT included:** NF-A4x10 5V (plain), NF-A4x20 5V, NF-A4x20 5V PWM, NF-A6x25 5V, NF-A6x25 5V
    PWM — these ship with the OmniJoin set and/or a 3:2-pin adaptor cable instead, not a USB cable.
  - When powered straight from USB, a 5V fan runs at fixed maximum speed with no speed control;
    adding an **NA-FC1** (PWM fans only) restores manual speed adjustment in this setup.
- **Powering 12V fans from a wall socket:** Noctua's **NV-PS1** power supply (230V/115V input,
  interchangeable EU/US/UK plugs) plus its **NA-AC10** barrel-to-fan connector can drive 12V Noctua
  fans directly from mains, without a full PC PSU.
- **Generic/third-party alternative:** plain "USB-A to 3-pin/2-pin fan connector" adaptor cables
  are widely sold by third parties (Amazon, AliExpress, etc.) for powering any 5V-rated fan from a
  USB port, power bank, or phone charger. These are functionally similar to Noctua's NA-AC7 but are
  not Noctua-branded; their build quality, wire gauge, and connector retention vary by seller and
  were not independently verified for this document — treat any specific such product's specs as
  `unknown` unless verified directly.
- Source: [FAQ: Can I power fans via a wall socket or USB?][usb-faq]
- **KUOQIY USB-A → 3/4-pin PWM fan adapter cable** (5-pack, amazon.nl ASIN B0D1C36WKB, ≈€7.98/5,
  5 V, 30 cm, black): confirmed by the fan-switch ticket (issue #32) that this specific product's
  fan-side connector mates **directly** with the `NF-A4x10 5V`'s own native 3-pin connector — no
  OmniJoin adaptor or NA-AC2 3:2-pin adaptor cable needed for this cable, unlike the general
  "generic third-party adaptor cables ... not independently verified" caveat above, which this
  narrows for this one specific product only. Chosen as this repo's decoder fan-power lead
  (`.claude/knowledge/architecture.md` §5, `docs/plans/2026-09-09-fan-switch.md` §3) because the
  plain `NF-A4x10 5V` ships no USB cable of its own (see the table above). Amazon fetches for this
  ASIN returned HTTP 500 during this pass — the spec above is per the ticket's own citation, not
  independently re-verified from the listing.

## Recommendation

Target: internal enclosure footprint ≈100 × 70 mm, 5–15 W of heat to remove, touring/live-production
use (quiet operation matters — avoid anything audible over normal stage ambient noise), simple
mounting.

| Priority | Fan (model + variant) | Why | Trade-off to accept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **NF-A6x25 5V PWM** (or plain 5V) | 60×60×25 mm frame and 50×50 mm mounting spacing fit comfortably inside a 100×70 mm footprint with room to spare; 29.2 m³/h max airflow gives large thermal margin over a 5–15 W load, meaning it can be run well below full speed (via PWM + NA-FC1, or a resistor/LNA-style voltage drop) for near-silent operation while still moving more air than the smaller fans at full tilt; 19.3 dB(A) max is already quiet, and running it under-driven for a light 5–15 W load would be quieter still. | Neither the 5V nor 5V PWM variant ships with a USB power adaptor cable — needs its own regulated 5V feed (likely available from the converter's own PSU) or the OmniJoin/2-pin adaptor route. Uses NA-AV1 mounts, not the more flexible NA-AV3. |
| **Secondary** | **NF-A4x20 5V PWM** | Lowest max noise of all variants reviewed (14.9 dB(A)); slim 40×40×20 mm footprint and 32×32 mm mounting spacing make it trivial to fit and mount (with NA-AV3 anti-vibration mounts for extra vibration decoupling) in a tighter internal layout if the 100×70 mm space is partly used by cabling/PCB; 9.4 m³/h airflow is adequate headroom for a 5–15 W load in a well-vented enclosure. | Meaningfully less airflow than the A6x25 (9.4 vs 29.2 m³/h) — fine as a thermal margin for 5–15 W, but leaves less headroom if the enclosure is tightly sealed or ambient touring-venue temperatures run hot. Also no bundled USB cable. |

**General trade-offs:**
- **5V + USB power adaptor cable** (NF-A4x10 5V PWM, NF-A8 5V/5V PWM): simplest possible power
  story — plug into any USB-A 5V source, no extra regulator needed — but fixed to max speed (loud
  end of the range) unless you add an NA-FC1, and the A8 size (80×80 mm) will not fit a 70 mm-wide
  enclosure dimension.
- **5V without bundled USB cable** (NF-A4x20, NF-A6x25 5V families): still just needs a plain
  5V rail, which a case design already providing 5V for the Magewell unit's own logic likely has
  available; slightly more design/wiring effort than a drop-in USB cable.
- **12V variants** (FLX/PWM): give access to Noctua's Low-Noise/Ultra-Low-Noise Adaptors for very
  quiet stepped-down speeds (e.g. NF-A8 FLX at 7.9 dB(A) with ULNA), but require a separate
  regulated 12V supply that a compact NDI converter case is unlikely to already have, adding a
  power-design complication most builds would rather avoid for a 5–15 W cooling load.
- **Airflow vs. noise:** for a light 5–15 W load, all four families reviewed are physically capable
  of adequate cooling; the deciding factors are really footprint fit (rules out the 80 mm NF-A8x25
  for a 70 mm-wide interior) and how quiet the fan needs to run at partial/idle speed for a
  stage environment — favoring the A6x25 (best airflow margin, allows heavy under-driving) or
  A4x20 (quietest at full speed) over the A4x10 (adequate but the least airflow margin of the
  useful sizes).
